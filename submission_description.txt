This Week 3 report presents a statistical analysis and hypothesis-testing study using the Netflix Movies and TV Shows dataset. The research question is whether the average duration of Netflix movies is greater than a 90-minute benchmark. The benchmark is treated as a statistical reference value rather than a claim about the worldwide average movie duration.

The analysis first filters the dataset to Movies because movie duration is consistently measured in minutes, unlike TV-show duration, which is represented in seasons. Numeric movie durations are extracted from the original text values, and descriptive statistics are calculated before hypothesis testing. The final analytical sample contains 6,126 movie-duration observations. Its mean is approximately 99.58 minutes, the median is 98 minutes, and the standard deviation is approximately 28.29 minutes.

The null hypothesis states that the mean movie duration is 90 minutes, while the alternative hypothesis states that the mean is greater than 90 minutes. A significance level of 0.05 is used. A one-sample t-test compares the observed sample mean with the benchmark. The resulting t-statistic is approximately 26.50 and the one-sided p-value is extremely small, providing strong evidence against the null hypothesis. The 95% confidence interval for the mean is approximately 98.87 to 100.29 minutes, and therefore the entire interval lies above 90 minutes.

Because movie durations are not perfectly normally distributed, a non-parametric Wilcoxon signed-rank result is also discussed as a robustness check. A published analysis of the same standard dataset reports a consistent conclusion.

The report includes the hypothesis, methodology, descriptive statistics, statistical results, confidence-interval interpretation, visualizations, robustness discussion, limitations and reproducible Python code. The overall conclusion is that the Netflix movie sample provides strong statistical evidence that its average duration is greater than 90 minutes, while the result should be interpreted within the limitations of the selected benchmark and dataset.
